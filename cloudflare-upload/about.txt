# About the Interactive Loot Map

## Built by a Fan, for the Fans

The **Interactive Loot Map will not be placed behind a paywall**.

This project uses AI-assisted tools when building parts of its infrastructure, particularly where they help keep development and hosting costs as low as possible.

Because of that, and because **Days Gone and its intellectual property do not belong to me**, it does not feel right to charge people simply to access the map or the information collected through it.

This is a fan project.

It is built by a fan, for other fans.

The goal is to make useful Days Gone information easier to access, not to take community knowledge about somebody else's game and lock it behind a subscription.

That does not mean the project requires no work.

Researching locations, verifying information, maintaining the database, improving the website, correcting mistakes, creating guides, and building new tools all require a substantial amount of time and effort.

I also have a day job, other responsibilities, other interests, and a life outside the broken roads of Oregon.

So please keep that in mind if development slows down, updates arrive in bursts, or I take a break from the project.

There may be periods where a lot happens very quickly.

There may also be periods where very little changes.

That does not necessarily mean the project has been abandoned.

This is something being built because I enjoy working on it and because I believe it can be useful to the Days Gone community — not because I owe anyone a release schedule.

**No paywalls. No promises of constant updates. Just a fan project that will keep growing when time allows.**

# What Is the Interactive Loot Map?

The Interactive Loot Map is an **interactive map and research database for Days Gone**.

There are already plenty of Days Gone loot maps, collectible guides, spreadsheets, videos, forum posts, Discord discussions, and community-made resources.

The problem is not that information does not exist.

The problem is that it exists **everywhere**.

Different resources document different things.

One map might show crafting materials.

Another guide might contain several reliable grenade locations.

A video may demonstrate where ammunition can be found.

A community member may have spent hours cataloguing loot in one particular region.

But there has never really been a single detailed location database that attempts to bring **all of that practical world information together in one place**.

That is what the Interactive Loot Map is trying to become.

---

# What the Map Currently Tracks

The map currently tracks crafting materials, healing supplies, weapons, throwables, plants, and other useful pickups throughout Oregon.

- Individual item filters
- One-time and respawnable pickup information where it is known
- Zoomed-out area summaries that expand into individual pickup locations

The current focus is on precise loot locations in Cascade Wilderness, Belknap, and Iron Butte, with more regions being added over time.

Want a different challenge? Try [Hide & Deek, the Days Gone Geo Guesser](https://interactivemap.baconfmspeedruns.workers.dev/hide-deek/).

# Why Make Another Loot Map?

Because the existing problem is not simply finding a map.

It is finding the **specific information you actually need**.

Imagine you are looking for one particular crafting material.

You could search old Reddit posts.

You could look through videos.

You could check community spreadsheets.

You could visit several different loot maps.

Or you could drive around Oregon opening every car trunk, searching every building, and hoping the item you need happens to be there.

That works.

Eventually.

But it is not a particularly good way of finding information.

For new and returning players especially, Days Gone contains an enormous amount of loot scattered throughout the world, and very little of it is comprehensively documented in one place.

The Interactive Loot Map is intended to reduce that searching.

Instead of wondering:

> “Where can I possibly find one of these?”

You should eventually be able to open the map, select the item, and immediately see the locations that are currently known.

# The Real Problem Is Showing Everything

A lot of community members have already tried to solve this problem.

And that creates another challenge.

**Days Gone has too much stuff.**

If every known item is simply placed onto one map at the same time, the result quickly becomes unusable.

Hundreds or thousands of markers might technically contain more information, but they do not necessarily help the person looking at them.

So one of the main challenges of the Interactive Loot Map is not simply collecting the data.

It is figuring out how to present a huge amount of information in a way that remains understandable.

That means experimenting with things such as:

- Item-specific filtering
- Categories and subcategories
- Search
- Marker grouping
- Zoom-dependent information
- Different marker types
- Respawn information
- One-time pickups
- Detailed location information
- Region-based organisation
- Hiding information that is not currently relevant

The goal is not simply to create the map with the most markers.

The goal is to create a map where **having more information does not make the map worse to use**.

# What Kind of Information Is Being Documented?

The project focuses heavily on practical world information.

That can include things such as:

- Crafting materials
- Throwables
- Explosives
- Ammunition
- Weapons
- Consumables
- Loot containers
- Car trunks
- Environmental pickups
- One-time items
- Respawning items
- Useful resource locations
- Speedrun-relevant locations
- Unusual or easily missed pickups
- Other useful discoveries found through research

Where possible, I also want the database to describe **how a location behaves**, rather than only saying that an item exists there.

For example:

- Does the item respawn?
- Is it a one-time pickup?
- Is it always there?
- Does story progression affect it?
- Is it inside a container?
- Is the location particularly difficult to describe?
- Is the information confirmed or still being researched?

Those details can be just as important as the marker itself.

# Who Is This For?

## New Players

Days Gone can be overwhelming when you do not yet know where useful resources are.

If you need a particular item, the Interactive Loot Map should help you find it without requiring detailed prior knowledge of the world.

## Returning Players

Maybe you have not played Days Gone in years.

You remember that certain items exist, but not which camp, building, roadside stop, NERO site, or abandoned car contained them.

The map should function as a quick reference without requiring you to relearn the entire world.

## Survival Players

Resource knowledge becomes considerably more important when fast travel and other conveniences disappear.

Knowing what is available along your route can prevent unnecessary trips and make preparation much easier.

## Challenge Runners

Challenge runs often create very specific resource problems.

You may need a particular explosive, crafting ingredient, weapon, or environmental resource while deliberately refusing to use easier alternatives.

Being able to find those resources quickly can make experimentation much less tedious.

## Speedrunners

A lot of the thinking behind this project comes directly from speedrunning.

For a speedrunner, a loot location is not simply useful.

Its location has a **cost**.

An item might be valuable because it sits directly beside a mission route.

Another technically identical item might be useless because collecting it requires a two-minute detour.

Having detailed map data makes it possible to start looking at resources as part of route development rather than isolated pickups.

## People Who Simply Want to Explore

You do not need to be routing anything.

If you enjoy exploring Days Gone, learning where things are, or discovering strange details about the world, this project is also for you.

# Community Knowledge

The Interactive Loot Map is not built around the idea that one person discovered everything.

The Days Gone community has accumulated an enormous amount of knowledge over the years.

Players have created maps, spreadsheets, guides, videos, posts, screenshots, notes, and countless Discord messages containing useful information.

A major goal of this project is to make that kind of knowledge easier to preserve and use.

Community members can contribute information through **Discord**.

That can include:

- Missing locations
- Corrections
- New discoveries
- Respawn behaviour
- Item information
- Screenshots
- Research results
- Guide corrections
- Other useful Days Gone knowledge

If something is wrong, it should be corrected.

If something is missing, it should eventually be added.

And if nobody knows the answer yet, that is a perfectly good excuse to test it.

---

# Research and Accuracy

This is an ongoing research project.

That means the database will not always be perfect.

Days Gone is a large game, and documenting individual pickups across the entire world is a substantial task.

Some areas will be more complete than others.

Some items may need additional testing.

Some assumptions may eventually turn out to be wrong.

When that happens, the information should change.

I would rather have a database that improves through testing and community corrections than one that presents uncertain information as permanent fact.

The map therefore represents the **best currently documented information**, not a claim that every corner of Oregon has already been completely catalogued.

# More Than a Loot Map

Although loot documentation is at the centre of the project, the Interactive Loot Map is also becoming the foundation for other Days Gone resources.

The broader goal is to connect information rather than create a collection of isolated pages.

Future additions include:

## Mission Guides

Detailed guides for specific missions, including useful information such as:

- Important objectives
- Useful loot along the route
- Easy-to-miss interactions
- Relevant mechanics
- Trophy requirements
- Alternate approaches
- Challenge considerations
- Speedrun information

## Platinum Trophy Guide

A complete Days Gone Platinum resource designed to make it clear what needs to be done, when it can be done, and how progress can be organised efficiently.

## Individual Trophy Guides

Dedicated guides for trophies that require additional explanation, planning, or tracking.

## Interactive Checklists

Checklists for tracking things such as:

- Trophy progress
- Collectibles
- Objectives
- Missions
- Completion requirements
- Other useful progression goals

## Speedrun Route Visualisation

One of the long-term goals is to make it possible to document:

- Speedrun routes
- Mission paths
- Important waypoints
- Resource pickups
- Optional detours
- Alternative strategies
- Category differences

This could turn the map into both a reference tool and a visual way of explaining how routes actually work.

## Better Connections Between Everything

Eventually, a mission guide should not have to exist separately from the map.

A trophy should not have to exist separately from a mission.

A checklist should not have to exist separately from a guide.

If a mission contains an important trophy requirement, useful pickup, collectible, or route decision, those pieces of information should be able to connect to one another.

That is one of the biggest long-term goals of the project.

# Is the Interactive Loot Map Finished?

No.

And realistically, a project like this may never have a perfect definition of *finished*.

You can define completion for a collectible list.

It is much harder to define completion when your question is:

> “Where is everything?”

Someone will always find another box behind a building.

Another trunk.

Another bottle.

Another grenade.

Another strange spawn.

Another location that somehow everyone has driven past for years.

And as the project expands into mission guides, trophies, checklists, routes, and other tools, the definition becomes even broader.

The aim is not to reach some arbitrary final version.

The aim is to keep improving the project whenever there is useful information to add or a better way to present it.
